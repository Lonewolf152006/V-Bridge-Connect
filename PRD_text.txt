Executive SummaryThe platform is a single system for managing college projects, assignments, departmental activities, competitions, clubs, research work, and industry-sponsored initiatives. Its primary job is coordination — not content delivery. It gives students clarity on what to do next and gives mentors a reliable view of progress, risks, submissions, and pending decisions.Recommended product shapeOne activity template that can represent a project, assignment, event, club initiative, research task, internship activity, or competition.A lifecycle from draft → published → applications/enrolment → active work → review → completed/archived.A mentor dashboard based on exceptions: overdue milestones, unanswered queries, pending reviews, inactive teams, and blocked work.A student's “My Work” view that combines deadlines, milestones, meetings, feedback, and announcements across activities.A restricted guest experience for industry partners, with access only to projects and teams explicitly shared with them.Certificates in one place — platform-issued on completion, and self-declared achievements a student adds themselves — always visually distinguished.In-workspace messaging — group chat per activity/team and direct chat with a mentor, professor, coordinator, or shared industry partner — scoped to shared workspaces, never a general-purpose messenger.MVP RECOMMENDATIONPilot project and activity management first, including certificates and messaging as native features. Avoid trying to replace the college LMS, ERP, attendance system, placement portal, or external chat applications in version one.2. Goals, Non-Goals, and Success Measures3.1 Product goalsReduce manual follow-up and reporting effort for mentors and coordinators.Give every student a clear, personalised view of current work and next actions.Standardise activity setup, approvals, milestones, submissions, reviews, and closure.Enable accountable collaboration between students, faculty, and approved external partners.Create a trustworthy record of participation, feedback, deliverables, outcomes, certificates, and communication.3.2 Non-goals for the MVPReplacing the full learning management system or hosting recorded courses.Replacing the college ERP, fee management, examination, payroll, or admissions systems.Building a general-purpose external messenger or a video-conferencing platform; in-app chat is scoped to activity/team workspaces and shared relationships only (see FR-120–FR-126).Automatically grading subjective project work with AI.Opening student data or internal discussions broadly to external partners.Verifying or approving self-declared achievement certificates — they are self-reported by design (see FR-112–FR-115).3.3 Proposed pilot success metricsMetricPilot targetHow measuredMentor follow-up effort≥30% reductionBefore/after weekly self-reported time spent chasing updates and files.On-time milestone completion≥80%Milestones completed by due date across pilot activities.Review turnaroundMedian ≤3 working daysTime from valid submission to first mentor review.Weekly active use≥75% of pilot usersMeaningful weekly action: update, submission, review, comment, or approval. Product PrinciplesEach principle below is a non-negotiable rule that should survive any downstream design, API, or implementation discussion.IDPrincipleStatementPR-01One source of truthImportant instructions, dates, files, decisions, and reviews live against the relevant activity or milestone, not scattered across channels.PR-02Next action is always visibleEach user can see what they owe, what is waiting on someone else, and what is overdue.PR-03Manage by exceptionMentors see risks and pending decisions first, not a wall of routine updates.PR-04Flexible but structuredDifferent initiatives share the same basic lifecycle while allowing custom forms, milestones, and rubrics.PR-05Transparent accountabilityOwnership, timestamps, changes, approvals, and review history are recorded for every critical transition.PR-06Safe external collaborationIndustry participation is valuable, but sharing must be deliberate, visible, and revocable.PR-07Communication stays in contextChat lives inside the activity/team workspace it belongs to; the platform is not a general-purpose messenger and never initiates messaging between users with no shared activity.PR-08Certificates are trustworthy by defaultA platform-issued credential and a self-declared achievement are never displayed, exported, or counted the same way.6. Critical End-to-End Workflows8.1 Faculty creates and runs a projectMentor selects a project template and enters scope, eligibility, team rules, milestones, rubric, and dates.If required, coordinator reviews and approves it; the system records requested changes and approval history.Eligible students discover and apply; mentor/coordinator shortlists or enrols participants.Teams are confirmed and receive their workspace, milestones, resources, chat, and communication channels.Students post progress and submit evidence; exceptions appear on the mentor dashboard.Mentor reviews work, requests changes where needed, and completes the rubric/final feedback.Coordinator closes the project; outcome, participation record, platform-issued certificate, and permitted portfolio evidence are retained.8.2 Student completes an assignment/activityStudent sees the item in My Work with due date, instructions, evaluation method, and required format.Student completes milestones or acknowledges instructions, asks contextual questions via chat or comments, and submits.System validates required fields and confirms the submitted version and timestamp.Mentor reviews; student receives feedback and a clear next state: accepted, changes requested, or evaluated.If changes are allowed, student resubmits without deleting prior versions; completion and certificate eligibility are recorded.8.3 Industry-sponsored projectApproved partner submits a structured brief and indicates confidentiality, data, IP, meeting, and evaluation expectations.Coordinator reviews feasibility, policy, ownership, and disclosure needs before publication.Students apply and teams are selected; partner receives access only after team confirmation and explicit sharing.Faculty remains the academic owner; partner gives domain feedback at approved milestones, via workspace comments or direct chat where shared.Final deliverables are shared according to permissions; academic evaluation stays controlled by the college.8.4 Blocker and escalationStudent/team marks work blocked, selects a category, explains impact, and requests help from the appropriate owner.Mentor receives a high-priority item; response expectation is visible.If unresolved past a configurable threshold, the coordinator is notified with context — not just a generic alert.Resolution, decision, owner, and any deadline change are recorded in the workspace.9. Analytics and Reporting DefinitionsA “meaningful activity” is a milestone/status update, submission, review, decision, blocker response, meeting action, or substantive comment — not merely opening a page, and not a chat message on its own.An “inactive team” has no meaningful activity within the configurable window for its activity type and current phase.Review turnaround starts at a valid submission and ends at the first formal review decision; time awaiting student correction is tracked separately.On-time completion uses the effective due date recorded when the submission occurred; extensions remain visible in history.Dashboards must show data freshness, filters, denominator, and excluded records so figures are interpretable.A platform-issued certificate counts toward completion metrics; a self-declared achievement never does.19. Risks and MitigationsRiskImpactMitigationScope becomes “everything college-related”Slow delivery, confusing UX, weak adoption.Start with project/activity coordination; publish explicit MVP boundaries and integration roadmap.Mentors see the app as extra workLow-quality updates and parallel spreadsheets continue.Design exception-first dashboards, minimise required fields, support templates/imports, and measure time saved.Students receive too many notificationsAlerts are ignored.Use action-based alerts, digests, preferences, quiet hours, and escalation thresholds.Sensitive data exposed to partnersPrivacy, trust, and institutional risk.Least privilege, explicit sharing, time-bound access, field-level separation, access review, and audit trails.Progress metrics become surveillanceGaming and unfair evaluation.Use system signals as prompts for conversation; never equate clicks, messages, or time online with contribution.Custom workflows become unmaintainableHigh support burden and inconsistent reporting.Offer governed templates and limited configuration; require admin review for new workflow types.Duplicate systems cause inconsistent truthUsers do not know which record to trust.Define ownership by data type and integrate incrementally; do not duplicate ERP/LMS functions without a migration plan.Self-declared certificates are misrepresentedA student's unverified claim could be mistaken for an institutional credential.Mandatory “Self-reported” labelling; automatic exclusion from official transcripts, exports, and completion records.In-app chat becomes an ungoverned parallel recordImportant academic decisions happen in chat instead of the tracked workspace, undermining the single source of truth.Chat stays scoped to shared workspaces only, is retained per policy, is visible to coordinators for audit, and is never counted as “meaningful activity” on its own.20. Pilot and Rollout PlanDiscovery (2–3 weeks)Interview 5–8 students, 4–6 faculty mentors, 1–2 coordinators, and 2–3 industry partners; map one current project flow and one activity flow; collect real forms, spreadsheets, rubrics, reports, and notification samples.Prototype (2 weeks)Test clickable flows for activity creation, My Work, workspace, submission, review, mentor exceptions, certificates, messaging, and partner sharing. Validate terminology and required fields.MVP buildImplement the agreed pilot scope, seed templates, migrate only necessary roster/activity data, run permission/security tests, and prepare short role-based onboarding.Controlled pilot (6–8 weeks)Use one department or 3–5 activities with named champions. Hold weekly feedback review; track metrics and parallel-system usage.Evaluation and expansionCompare outcomes with baseline, fix high-friction workflows, decide integrations, and expand by department with governance and support ownership.21.
